TITLE: Surgery Cancellation Policy
DOCUMENT ID: POL-CANCEL-001
EFFECTIVE DATE: 2025-01-01
OWNER: Department of Surgical Operations

--- PAGE 1 ---

SECTION 1: PURPOSE

This policy defines the conditions under which a scheduled surgery may be
cancelled, the notice periods required, and the documentation that must
accompany a cancellation request. It applies to all elective, urgent, and
emergency surgical procedures scheduled through the Hospital Surgery
Management system.

SECTION 2: SCOPE

This policy applies to scheduling staff, surgeons, and operations
coordinators who have authority to request or approve the cancellation of
a scheduled surgery.

--- PAGE 2 ---

SECTION 3: CANCELLATION ELIGIBILITY

A surgery may be cancelled under the following conditions:

3.1 The surgery has a status of "scheduled", "confirmed", or "delayed".
    Surgeries with a status of "completed" can never be cancelled.
3.2 The surgery has not yet transitioned to "in_progress". A surgery that
    has started cannot be cancelled; it may only be marked as completed or
    delayed by the attending surgical team.
3.3 A documented reason is recorded with every cancellation request,
    including but not limited to: patient-initiated cancellation, medical
    contraindication identified during pre-operative assessment, surgeon
    unavailability, or operating room unavailability due to equipment
    failure.

SECTION 4: NOTICE PERIODS

4.1 Routine elective surgeries: cancellation should be requested at least
    24 hours in advance where possible, although the system does not block
    cancellations requested with shorter notice.
4.2 Urgent surgeries: may be cancelled at any time prior to the start of
    the procedure if a qualifying medical or logistical reason is
    recorded.
4.3 Emergency surgeries: cancellation is permitted only when the treating
    surgeon confirms the procedure is no longer clinically indicated.

--- PAGE 3 ---

SECTION 5: APPROVAL AND CONFIRMATION

5.1 Every cancellation is a state-changing action. The system (including
    any AI assistant) must present the proposed cancellation -- including
    the surgery identifier, scheduled time, and eligibility assessment --
    to the requesting staff member and obtain explicit confirmation before
    the cancellation is executed.
5.2 Cancellations must never be executed automatically from a single
    natural-language request without a confirmation step.
5.3 Every cancellation must generate an audit event recording the actor,
    timestamp, previous status, new status, and reason.

SECTION 6: POST-CANCELLATION HANDLING

6.1 A cancelled surgery remains in the system with a status of
    "cancelled" for audit and reporting purposes. Records are never
    deleted as part of a routine cancellation.
6.2 If the patient still requires the procedure, a new surgery should be
    scheduled rather than attempting to reactivate a cancelled record.

--- PAGE 4 ---

SECTION 7: EXAMPLES

Example A: A routine knee replacement scheduled for next week is
cancelled by the patient two days in advance. This is a standard
cancellation; no special approval is required beyond standard
confirmation.

Example B: A same-day urgent cardiac procedure is cancelled because the
surgeon identifies a new contraindication during pre-operative review.
This is permitted provided the reason is documented and confirmation is
obtained from the requesting clinician.

Example C: A request to cancel a surgery that is already marked
"completed" must be rejected, since completed procedures cannot be
cancelled under Section 3.1.

END OF DOCUMENT.
